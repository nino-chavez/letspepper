# Let's Pepper Voice and Copy Gate

## Position

Let's Pepper sounds like someone who plays, watches, and understands the game.
The voice is direct, observant, competitive, and warm without becoming corporate
or performing youth slang.

The test: **would a player repost this without having to excuse the caption?**

## What the voice does

- Names the real moment: ace, roof, pancake, comeback, match point.
- Gives useful context: event, round, team, score, gallery, next action.
- Celebrates the winner without turning the losing player into content.
- Lets photography and footage carry emotion instead of over-writing it.
- Uses volleyball language naturally and pepper language selectively.
- Invites participation with a clear action: vote, pick, tag, watch, register.

## What the voice does not do

- It does not imitate whatever slang is currently popular.
- It does not call women queens, girls, baddies, or any other patronizing shorthand.
- It does not call players unhinged, menaces to society, washed, chokers, victims, or frauds.
- It does not joke about injury, bodies, ability, ethnicity, gender, sexuality, age, or mental health.
- It does not reward playing through injury, skipping recovery, or ignoring a player's limits.
- It does not manufacture beef or imply personal hostility between teams.
- It does not use sexualized character design or copy to create engagement.
- It does not stack pepper puns. One is enough; zero is often better.

## Register

### Headlines

Short, concrete, and readable in under one second.

Good:

- `MATCH POINT`
- `UPSET ALERT`
- `FINAL STANDINGS`
- `FIELD SET`
- `ONE MORE EVENT`
- `THE SEASON CLOSES HERE`

Avoid:

- `THEY ABSOLUTELY COOKED`
- `BRO IS DIFFERENT`
- `SHE ATE AND LEFT NO CRUMBS`
- `POV: YOU JUST GOT DESTROYED`
- `THIS TEAM IS A MENACE TO SOCIETY`

### Captions

Use this order when it fits:

1. One specific observation.
2. Event and match context.
3. One useful destination or prompt.
4. Credit.

Example:

> Three touches, no wasted movement. Bell Pepper Open · Aurora, IL. Full gallery at letspepper.com/gallery. Shot by @flickday.media.

### Calls to action

Prefer exact destinations over platform filler.

- `REGISTER AT LETSPEPPER.COM`
- `FULL GALLERY → LETSPEPPER.COM/GALLERY`
- `MAKE YOUR PICK`
- `VOTE NOW`
- `TAG YOUR TEAM`

`LINK IN BIO` is acceptable only when the URL cannot be made clickable and the
destination is actually current. It should not be baked into evergreen assets.

## Slang policy

Players may use slang in quoted UGC. Brand-authored copy should not lead with:

- `it's giving`, `no cap`, `rizz`, `slay`, `ate`, `delulu`
- `main character`, `built different`, `rent free`, `understood the assignment`
- `low-key`, `high-key`, `the vibes are immaculate`, `good vibes only`
- `unhinged`, `menace to society`, `absolute cinema`

These are not banned because they are morally wrong; they are excluded because
brand usage usually arrives late and ages quickly.

## Competition and sensitivity

Use sport terms such as `kill` and `stuff block` only when the volleyball context
is obvious. Prefer `finish`, `point`, or `put-away` in general-audience placements.

Never frame an error as the identity of a player. `SERVE ERROR` may describe a
scorebook event; `CHOKE` may not describe a person or team.

For women's events, use the same competitive register as every other event:

- `Women's grass triples`
- `Built for women who compete`
- `Same format. Its own field.`

Do not feminize the entire visual system or rely on royal/beauty language.

## Emoji and punctuation

- Zero to two emoji in a caption; none is the default for graphics.
- Do not replace nouns with emoji in information-heavy posts.
- Use periods and em dashes. Avoid rows of exclamation points.
- All caps is a visual system for headlines, not an emotional volume control.

## Approval labels

- `approved` — production-ready.
- `context` — valid only in the documented setting.
- `revise` — the idea is usable but the phrase should not ship.
- `blocked` — not appropriate for brand-authored copy.
