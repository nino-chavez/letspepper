# Let's Pepper creative system strategy

## Design judgment

The established direction is strong: dark sports editorial layouts, mono utility type,
compressed display headlines, restrained heat colors, real photography, and gritty
anime peppers. The weak point was not the taste level of individual hero images. It
was that too much value lived in finished one-off art while the weekly production job
needed small, repeatable parts.

The v1 system fixes that by separating five layers:

1. **Identity** — logo, mascot family, palette, type, sonic logo.
2. **Broadcast utility** — bugs, lower thirds, scores, trackers, credits, LUTs.
3. **Editorial moments** — ace, dig, block, comeback, final point, photo, champion.
4. **Event lifecycle** — registration through gallery and final standings.
5. **Participation** — quiz results, picks, votes, bingo, player/season cards.

The footage remains the proof. Graphics orient, identify, and reward; they do not cover
the touch or compensate for an unidentified clip.

## What v1 now covers

- Persistent and momentary Reel overlays for both light and dark footage.
- A play-safe placement guide designed around the outdoor 9:16 CapCut use case.
- Alpha motion and Screen-mode fallbacks with original short audio cues.
- Poblano Open launch, live-day, and post-event lifecycle masters.
- Reel covers, Highlight covers, fan-share cards, quiz results, and link previews.
- A twelve-character mascot roster: four full pose families plus eight identity anchors.
- Website integration for quiz art, downloadable Story cards, and route-specific Open Graph art.
- Deterministic generators, manifests, contact sheets, and media preflight.

## Production operating model

### Before an event

- Lock verified event name, date, location, format, URL, sponsor obligations, and rights owner.
- Fill `clip-map-template.csv` for archival footage and prepare the roster/handle map.
- Render registration, field-set, schedule, weather, matchup, and “I’m in” assets.
- Assign one mascot role for the campaign; avoid putting the whole family on every post.

### During an event

- Use the bug at low opacity; use moment stamps for under one second after the point.
- Record court, teams, round, score, play, player, creator, and consent while the context is fresh.
- Publish factual updates faster than decorative posts: weather, schedule, bracket, results.
- Never post an identifiable low moment merely because it may drive comments.

### After an event

- Publish final standings, a specific play recap, photo of the day, full gallery, and participant cards.
- Tag players only from verified handles; credit the actual photographer or footage owner.
- Feed final metadata back into the clip map so the Reel archive becomes searchable.

## Next wave: data and product, not more decoration

These are the highest-value additions after v1:

1. **Clip identification console.** Thumbnail grid that assigns event, court, teams,
   round, score, play, handles, and rights status; then writes the queue caption and
   recommended overlay automatically.
2. **Live scorebug adapter.** Connect the existing broadcast UI to verified RallyHQ
   match data and export a browser-source or timed overlay. Never fake “live” state.
3. **Player media locker.** A page where a participant can find approved photos/Reels,
   download a Story card, and copy correctly credited post text.
4. **Team roster/handle registry.** One canonical identity record prevents misspellings,
   wrong tags, and inconsistent team naming across cards.
5. **Rights ledger.** Photographer, participant release, sponsor use, expiration, and
   allowed channels attached to every media item.
6. **Accessible media layer.** Human alt text, captions, high-contrast variants, and
   reduced-motion exports generated alongside every public asset.
7. **On-site system.** Court numbers, check-in QR, schedule board, weather/delay signs,
   photo notice, rules cards, volunteer identifiers, podium backdrop, and sponsor wall.
8. **Creator handoff kit.** Clean bug, credit rules, safe zones, three caption patterns,
   and upload naming rules for approved community creators.
9. **Sticker/GIF distribution.** Publish the small transparent marks, trackers, and
   mascot reactions to a searchable sticker channel only after trademark/partner review.
10. **Season data stories.** Auto-render podiums, point movement, giant killers,
    comeback paths, attendance, rally lengths, and photo coverage from verified data.

## Restraint rules

- Do not expand every new mascot into seven poses until it has a recurring content job.
- Do not place a large character on top of a live touch, face, ball, or net contact.
- Do not mix the orange, yellow, green, cyan, and red accents at equal weight.
- Do not turn every caption into a pepper pun.
- Do not let a partner credit compete with the event headline.
- Do not use slang because it appears current; specificity ages better.
- Do not manufacture conflict, humiliation, or unsafe effort as engagement bait.
